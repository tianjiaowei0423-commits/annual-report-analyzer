# Credit summaries, 2025

Written by Claude from the ratio table in `output/ratios.xlsx`. The underlying figures were extracted from the annual reports and checked against a hand-made ground truth.

## DFDS

**DFDS is the most stretched of the four: leverage is high, EBIT does not cover interest, and the year ended in a net loss, though cash generation is positive.**

- **Profitability:** The EBITDA margin is 12.1%, but after depreciation the EBIT margin falls to 1.7%. Net profit is DKK -425m, a loss.
- **Debt:** NIBD / EBITDA is 4.1x, above the level usually seen as high (around 3x). Interest cover is 0.7x, below 1.0x, so EBIT does not cover interest at all. The equity ratio of 36.2% is the lowest in the group.
- **Cash:** Cash conversion is 88.2% and capex intensity is 5.1%. Operating cash flow (DKK 3,300m) exceeds capex (DKK 1,576m), giving a FCF margin of 5.6% on the common definition. The company reports FCF of DKK 2,957m against DKK 1,724m on the common definition, because companies define free cash flow differently.
- **Watch:** Interest cover of 0.7x combined with NIBD / EBITDA of 4.1x: earnings need to improve, or debt come down, for the balance sheet to loosen.

## Maersk

**Maersk has the strongest balance sheet in the group: net cash, 3.1x interest cover and a 64.2% equity ratio, with profit and cash flow both positive.**

- **Profitability:** The EBITDA margin is 17.7% and the EBIT margin is 6.5%. Net profit is USD 2,915m.
- **Debt:** NIBD is negative, so the company is in a net cash position. Interest cover is 3.1x, and the equity ratio is 64.2%.
- **Cash:** Cash conversion is 102.4%, so operating cash flow is above EBITDA. Capex intensity is 8.9%, and operating cash flow (USD 9,761m) exceeds capex (USD 4,799m), giving a FCF margin of 9.2%. Reported FCF of USD 2,232m is well below the USD 4,962m on the common definition, since companies define free cash flow differently.
- **Watch:** The gap between EBITDA margin (17.7%) and EBIT margin (6.5%) shows the weight of depreciation, and interest cover of 3.1x is lower than at Norden and TORM.

## Norden

**Norden has low leverage and comfortable interest cover, but heavy fleet investment pushed free cash flow negative on the common definition.**

- **Profitability:** The EBITDA margin is 14.5% and the EBIT margin is 5.1%. Net profit is USD 120.3m.
- **Debt:** NIBD / EBITDA is 0.8x, interest cover is 4.3x, and the equity ratio is 53.9%. Leverage is low and interest is well covered.
- **Cash:** Cash conversion is 83.2%, the lowest in the group. Capex intensity is 20.2%, and capex (USD 630.1m) exceeds operating cash flow (USD 378.2m), so FCF on the common definition is USD -251.9m, a FCF margin of -8.1%. The company reports FCF of USD 170.4m, a positive figure, because companies define free cash flow differently.
- **Watch:** Capex (USD 630.1m) is above operating cash flow (USD 378.2m); if investment stays at this level, net debt will have to rise to fund it.

## TORM

**TORM combines the highest margins in the group with moderate leverage, strong interest cover and positive free cash flow after heavy capex.**

- **Profitability:** The EBITDA margin is 42.6% and the EBIT margin is 26.6%, both the highest of the four. Net profit is USD 286m.
- **Debt:** NIBD / EBITDA is 1.5x, interest cover is 5.2x, the highest in the group, and the equity ratio is 65.4%.
- **Cash:** Cash conversion is 87.4% and capex intensity is 23.2%, the highest in the group. Operating cash flow (USD 498.9m) still exceeds capex (USD 310.3m), giving a FCF margin of 14.1%. Reported FCF of USD 346m is higher than the USD 188.6m on the common definition, because companies define free cash flow differently.
- **Watch:** Margins of 42.6% (EBITDA) and 26.6% (EBIT) are well above the other three, so credit metrics depend on these margins holding up.

## Peer comparison

Maersk has the strongest balance sheet (net cash, 64.2% equity ratio), while TORM has the highest margins (42.6% EBITDA, 26.6% EBIT) and the highest interest cover (5.2x). Norden has low leverage (0.8x) but is the only company with negative FCF on the common definition (-8.1% margin). DFDS is the weakest on leverage (4.1x), interest cover (0.7x) and equity ratio (36.2%), and it is the only one with a net loss. The business models differ (ferries and logistics, container shipping, dry bulk and tankers, product tankers), so part of these gaps comes from the business, not only from how well each company is run. Reported FCF differs from the common definition at all four companies, so the reported figures should not be compared directly.
